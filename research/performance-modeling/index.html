Research / Explainable & Generalizable Performance Modeling

Explainable & Generalizable Performance Modeling

Learned simulators and performance models that predict CPU/GPU performance quickly and explain why a design behaves the way it does.

Cycle-level simulators are too slow to explore modern design spaces, and black-box ML predictors are fast but rarely tell an architect why a design is slow. We build machine-learning-based simulators and performance models that are fast, accurate, generalizable, and explainable.

Our earlier work showed that deep learning can replace cycle-level simulation (SimNet, TAO) and scale it on GPUs, and that analytical and ML models can be fused for fast CPU performance modeling (Concorde, with Google). We now focus on models that generalize to unseen architectures, expose microarchitectural bottlenecks, and plug into existing simulators and LLM-based design agents.

Open questions

  • How can learned performance models generalize to architectures and workloads they have never seen?
  • Which bottlenecks (dependencies, critical paths, resource contention) should a model explain, and how do we know the explanation is faithful?
  • Can LLMs act as performance agents for architecture design-space exploration with only limited fine-tuning?

Current projects

Event-driven explainable simulation

Instead of mapping instructions straight to cycles, predict fine-grained microarchitectural events and derive cycles from them, exposing the bottleneck behind every prediction.

Hybrid analytical–ML models

Combine analytical models with learned components and architecture-consistency constraints (in the spirit of physics-informed learning) for label-efficient, generalizable prediction.

Modular and architecture-aware models

Architecture-aware mixtures of experts and component-level models that can be retrained separately as systems evolve.

LLM performance agents

Benchmarks of microarchitecture reasoning where today's LLMs fail, and LLM agents that guide architecture design with a performance model in the loop.

Learning from RTL

Train simulators on cycle-accurate RTL data (e.g., FPGA-accelerated simulation) rather than only cycle-level simulators, and build surrogate models of real processors.
